Asking for help

A clear request is easier to answer than a hint. A friend can offer company, and a trained person is the right ask when company is not enough.

Lesson 9 of 9 · General wellness education, not medical advice.

Learning goals

  • Ask for a specific kind of help instead of hoping someone will guess.
  • Recognise when a problem needs a trained person, not only a friend.
  • Practise being available to someone else without taking their life over.

A clear ask is kinder than a hint

Many of us were taught not to be a burden. So we sigh, go quiet, or say "I am fine" in a flat voice, and hope someone notices. Sometimes they do. Often they are tired too, or they have learned not to push. A clear ask is kinder to both people.

It helps to be specific. "Can you sit with me for twenty minutes tonight? I do not need advice." Or, "Can you help me phone the clinic tomorrow morning?" The request has a person, a task, a length, and an honest note about what you do not want. Hinting asks the other person to do the guessing as well as the helping.

Friends, and trained care

Friends and family are good for company, a meal, a lift, and being believed. They are not always the right place for ongoing despair, for violence at home, or for a condition that needs treatment. Clinics, counsellors, social workers, and helplines exist for that. Choosing them is not a rejection of your people. It keeps a friendship from having to be a whole health system.

In South Africa, SADAG can listen and help you find local mental-health resources on 0800 567 567. If you or someone with you is in immediate danger, contact local emergency services. A lesson can help you find the words. It cannot take the place of that call.

If someone tells you

Stay. You do not need a polished speech. Useful sentences are short. "Thank you for telling me." "I believe you." "Do you want company, or help finding a next step?" Avoid debate, comparison, and a quick motivational talk. The person has usually already argued with themselves.

If they talk about suicide or about harming themselves, do not keep it as a secret you carry alone. Stay with them while you contact a trusted adult, a clinic, or SADAG on 0800 567 567. You are allowed to be frightened and still make the call. You are not expected to know the clinical answer. You are expected not to disappear, and not to mock.

Community is repeated contact

Awareness fades when it is only a poster. It lives in who checks on a neighbour, who notices the colleague who stopped eating with anyone, and who lets "I am not okay" be said without a joke at that person's expense. In a town like Vryheid, as in any town, the people who notice first are usually not specialists. They are the ones already in the room.

Being that person is ordinary and serious. It is also allowed to have limits. You can help someone find care and still go home and sleep. Community is not the same thing as having no boundary of your own.

Try this

Draft one help sentence you could actually send. Name the person, the ask, how long it will take, and whether you want advice. If you do not need help this week, draft a check-in instead: one observation, one offer, and no pressure to reply. Send whichever sentence is true.

Recap

  • Specific requests are easier to answer than hints.
  • Friends are for company. Clinics and helplines are for care that needs training.
  • When someone tells you they are not coping, believe them and ask what they need.
  • If there is talk of harm, do not carry it alone. SADAG is 0800 567 567.
  • Community is made of ordinary check-ins, not only campaigns.

Reflection

  1. Who is realistically safe for you to ask, and what would you ask them for?
  2. What kind of help do you tend to refuse even when you need it?
  3. Who around you has gone quiet, and what is one low-pressure way to reach them?
