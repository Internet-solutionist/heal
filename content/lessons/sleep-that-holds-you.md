## Learning goals

- Describe sleep as maintenance for the body and the mind, not a reward you have to earn.
- Name two changes that make sleep more likely on an ordinary night.
- Notice one thing in your own evening that helps, and one that gets in the way.

## Sleep is maintenance

Sleep is not laziness, and it is not a prize for a productive day. While you sleep, the body repairs tissue, the immune system does quiet work, and the brain sorts what the day held. Most adults do better with something like seven to nine hours. Some people need a little less, and some a little more. If you wake unrefreshed on most days, the amount or the quality may be off. That is information about your sleep. It is not a verdict on your character.

A short night before an exam, a shift, or a sick child is a human night. The concern is the pattern: weeks of short or broken sleep, and days that feel dull, irritable, or unsafe because you are not properly awake.

## A steady time beats a strict plan

The body likes a rhythm. Going to bed and waking around the same time, including on days off when you can, trains that rhythm more reliably than a complicated routine you will abandon. Shift work, babies, load-shedding, and a late last taxi make this uneven. Do the part you can control. Protect a wind-down. Keep the wake time as steady as you can on the days that belong to you.

If you sleep very little during the week and then try to catch up in one long morning, you may feel groggy rather than restored. A slightly earlier night, repeated, usually helps more than one heroic lie-in.

## Light, caffeine, and the last hour

Morning light, even from the stoep or the walk to transport, tells the body that daytime has started. Caffeine can linger for hours. If sleep is fragile, keep coffee, strong tea, and energy drinks to the earlier part of the day. Alcohol can make you drowsy and still break up the second half of the night, so it is a poor sleep aid.

The trouble with screens is less about one special colour of light, and more about a mind that stays switched on. A dimmer room and a slower last half hour give sleep a chance: washing, a short stretch, a dull page, a conversation that is not a dispute. The phone can charge away from the pillow. If the room is shared and that is awkward, it can at least leave your hand.

## When lying awake makes it worse

Staring at the dark and arguing with yourself rarely brings sleep. If you have been awake for a long while, get up, keep the light low, and do something quiet until you feel sleepy, then go back to bed. A worry can go onto paper in two lines: what is on your mind, and the smallest next step, which is allowed to wait until morning.

Loud snoring with gasping, or sleepiness that makes the day unsafe, is a reason to speak to a clinic. So is a low mood that sits with sleeplessness for weeks. This lesson is general knowledge. It cannot tell you what is happening in your own case.

## Try this

Tonight, choose one change only. The phone charges away from the pillow. The last caffeine is before mid-afternoon. The lights go dimmer for half an hour before you lie down. Tomorrow's wake time is written down. Pick the one that fits your house, and try it for three nights before you decide whether it helped.

## Recap

- Sleep restores the body and the mind. It is part of health, not a luxury you must deserve.
- A roughly regular schedule helps more than chasing a perfect night.
- Morning light, less late caffeine, and a quieter last hour are practical levers.
- Severe sleep trouble, or sleeplessness with low mood for weeks, belongs in a conversation with a clinic.

## Reflection

1. On a typical night this week, what time did you actually fall asleep, and how did the next day feel?
2. Which one evening habit would be the easiest to change for the next three nights?
3. Who else in the home would need to know, so the change is not only yours to defend?
