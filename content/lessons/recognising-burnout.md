## Learning goals

- Name the usual cluster of burnout: exhaustion, distance, and a drop in effect.
- Separate ordinary tiredness from a longer depletion.
- Choose one recovery step and one change that reduces the load.

## More than a tiring week

Everyone has heavy weeks. Burnout is what accumulates when demand stays high and recovery stays low, often for months. People describe emptiness more than ordinary sleepiness, a cynicism about work or duty they used to care about, and a sense that effort produces less than it used to. The word is most often used for paid work. A similar pattern shows up in long caregiving and in study. If your role in the family is "the strong one", you may be the last to notice.

A weekend can help a tiring week. It rarely unwinds months of no recovery. That is not a reason to refuse the weekend. It is a reason not to expect the weekend to do the whole job.

## Signs worth trusting

Watch for dread before ordinary tasks, irritability that surprises you, and body complaints that do not settle, such as headaches, a sore stomach, or frequent colds. Watch for sleep that does not restore, for withdrawing from people you like, and for sugar, alcohol, or constant noise as the only way through. One sign on a bad day is life. Several signs, on most days, for weeks, are a pattern. Treat the pattern as data.

You can score a recent fortnight from 0 to 5 on three lines: exhaustion, distance or cynicism, and "my effort is not working". If two of those scores are 3 or higher, take the pattern seriously enough to tell someone.

## Rest, and a change in the load

A rest day is worth taking. Burnout usually also needs a change in the load: fewer hours if that can be negotiated, shared caregiving, a subject dropped, a boundary at work, or treatment if depression is part of the picture. "Be more resilient" adds a demand to a person who is already spent. Recovery is allowed to include a no.

If the emptiness includes constant hopelessness, or thoughts of harming yourself, this is more than a workload problem. Talk to a clinic, or to SADAG on 0800 567 567. You do not have to wait until you have the right explanation.

## A minimum that keeps you a person

While a bigger change is being arranged, protect a small minimum: one meal you sit down for, a sleep window you defend more often than not, one human contact that is not a task, and one activity that is not useful to anyone else. This is not indulgence. It is how a person stays able to care about anything at all.

If even the minimum feels impossible, that fact belongs in the conversation with whoever can change the load. It is evidence, not a further failure.

## Try this

Score the last fortnight from 0 to 5 on exhaustion, on distance or cynicism, and on "my effort is not working". If two scores are 3 or higher, tell one person a plain sentence: "I think I am burning out, and I need the load to change in this specific way." Name one change, even a small one, such as one evening a week that is not available.

## Recap

- Burnout builds from long demand and short recovery.
- Exhaustion, cynicism, and reduced effect are the usual cluster.
- Rest matters, and so does changing the load.
- A protected minimum keeps you from disappearing while you renegotiate.
- Hopelessness or thoughts of harm need a clinic or SADAG on 0800 567 567, not only a weekend off.

## Reflection

1. Which early sign have you been explaining away?
2. What part of your load is even slightly negotiable?
3. Which minimum, a meal, sleep, contact, or something that is just yours, have you dropped that you could put back this week?
