## Learning goals

- Tell a cover word such as "fine" or "stressed" apart from a more precise feeling.
- Practise noticing a feeling without immediately arguing with it or obeying it.
- Choose a small response that matches the feeling, including rest or company.

## Cover words hide the useful part

"Stressed", "fine", "busy", and "whatever" are covers. Under them might be disappointment, fear, anger, loneliness, shame, grief, or plain tiredness. Those are different situations. Tiredness needs rest or food. Anger may need a boundary. Loneliness needs contact. Shame often needs a kinder witness, not a talking-to. Grief may need time that nobody is hurrying.

A more precise word is not therapy jargon. It is a way to stop treating every discomfort with the same reaction.

## Noticing is not the same as obeying

You can notice "I am envious" without acting on envy. You can notice "I am frightened" without deciding that the fear is a full prediction of what will happen. Feelings carry information about what matters to you, and about what you think is going on. They can be early, exaggerated, or exactly right. The skill is a short pause: name it, then decide.

You also do not have to perform calm for other people while you do this. A quiet face is not the same thing as knowing what you feel.

## The body often speaks first

Heat in the face. A dropped stomach. A wish to leave the room. Tight shoulders. If the words are slow, start there. "Tight chest, and I think this is worry about tomorrow." If you still cannot find the word, "something is wrong and I do not have the name yet" is more honest than "I am fine" when you are not.

Other people sometimes rush to correct a feeling because they are uncomfortable. You are allowed to say, "I am not asking you to solve it. I am telling you."

## Match the next step to the feeling

If you are lonely, one message may be the right size. If you are overwhelmed, shrink the task in front of you. If you are grieving, you may not need a fix at all, only a place to be sad without being managed. If you are hungry or short of sleep, deal with that before you hold a summit with your personality.

The name does not have to be perfect to be useful. "Sad" is already more useful than "whatever" if sadness is what is there.

## Try this

Once today, when you notice a mood, write a single sentence: "I feel ____ because ____." If you do not know the because, write "and I do not know why yet." Do not add advice in the same sentence. Read it back once. That is the whole practice.

## Recap

- Precise names, such as tired, angry, lonely, or afraid, point toward different next steps.
- Noticing a feeling is not the same as obeying it.
- The body is often the first clue, before the word arrives.
- Some feelings need company or rest, not a solution.

## Reflection

1. Which cover word do you use most: fine, stressed, busy, or whatever?
2. What feeling was under it the last time you used that word?
3. Who can hear a feeling from you without immediately trying to fix it?
